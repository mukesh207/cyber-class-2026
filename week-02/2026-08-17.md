## 1. What is it?
**Wireless Pentesting** is the practice of assessing the security of networks that communicate over radio frequencies (RF) rather than physical cables. It involves evaluating Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11), Bluetooth, and IoT protocols. 

**Technical Definition:** It is the active evaluation of the physical, data link, and network layers of wireless infrastructure. This includes analyzing the electromagnetic spectrum (EMS), modulation techniques, and protocol implementations to identify vulnerabilities such as weak encryption, insecure management frames, or default credentials.

## 2. Why does it happen?
Wireless networks are uniquely vulnerable because the transmission medium is the **open air**. 
- An attacker does not need physical access to a building or a network switch; they only need to be within radio range.
- Over **70% of public Wi-Fi networks** have weak or no encryption, making them susceptible to Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) attacks.
- Legacy protocols (WEP and WPA1) are still used in ~5% of industrial/IoT environments.
- Newer standards (like WPA2) are still vulnerable to offline dictionary attacks if the pre-shared key (password) is weak.

## 3. How does it work?
To hack wireless, you must understand the physics of how radio waves carry data.

**Core Concepts:**
| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Frequency** | The number of waves passing a fixed point in one second (Hertz - Hz). |
| **Wavelength**| Physical distance between two peaks. Higher frequency = shorter wavelength. |
| **Amplitude** | Height of the wave, indicating signal strength/power. |
| **Bandwidth** | Range of frequencies used. Wider bandwidth = more data capacity. |

**Modulation:**
You cannot simply send raw data (the **Message Signal**) effectively over long distances. You need a **Carrier Signal** (a high-frequency blank wave). **Modulation** is the process of modifying the Carrier Signal with the Message Signal (e.g., varying frequency in FM - Frequency Modulation, while keeping amplitude constant).

```text
[Message Signal] \
                  \
                   +---> (Modulator) ----> [Transmitted Modulated Wave]
                  /                                 |
[Carrier Signal] /                                  v
                                           ((Repeater / Amplifier))
                                                    |
                                                    v
                                              (Demodulator)
                                                    |
                                                    v
                                           [Original Message]
```
*Why this works:* The high-frequency carrier wave travels well through the air and penetrates obstacles, while modulation embeds the 1s and 0s. A receiver demodulates it back into usable data.

## 4. Where is it used / found?
Wireless technology covers the entire **Electromagnetic Spectrum (EMS)**, predominantly within Radio Frequencies (RF).

### Common Wireless Technologies
| Tech | Freq Band | Pros / Cons | Pentest Target |
|------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Bluetooth** | 2.4 GHz | Low power / Short range (10m) | Bluejacking, Bluesnarfing. |
| **Wi-Fi** | 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz | High bandwidth / Interference prone | WPA2 cracking, Evil Twins. |
| **Cellular** | 700 MHz - 39 GHz| Global coverage / Centralized & expensive | IMSI catching (Stingrays), SS7 flaws. |
| **Zigbee** | 2.4 GHz (915 MHz)| Mesh network (IoT), low power / Low bandwidth | Smart home hubs (sniffing keys). |
| **NFC** | 13.56 MHz | Proximal security (4cm) / Zero range | Cloning keycards, skimming payments. |
| **RFID** | LF, HF, UHF | Passive (no battery) / Easily sniffed from afar | Stealing building access badges. |

### Wi-Fi Standards (IEEE 802.11 Framework)
| Standard | Wi-Fi Version | Freq Band | Key Pentest Weakness |
|----------|---------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **802.11b** | Wi-Fi 1 | 2.4 GHz | WEP encryption (crackable in 2 mins). |
| **802.11g** | Wi-Fi 3 | 2.4 GHz | WPA1 introduction (TKIP is vulnerable). |
| **802.11n** | Wi-Fi 4 | 2.4 / 5 GHz | WPS PINs are easily brute-forced via tools like Reaver. |
| **802.11ac** | Wi-Fi 5 | 5 GHz | Deauthentication attacks (unencrypted management frames). |
| **802.11ax** | Wi-Fi 6 | 2.4/5/6 GHz | WPA3 introduced. Mitigation requires downgrade attacks to WPA2. |

## 5. How do I identify it?
In wireless networks, you look for broadcasted management frames (like Beacons) which announce the presence of an access point. 

- **SSID:** The human-readable name of the network.
- **BSSID:** The MAC address of the access point.
- **Channels:** Specific frequency ranges within a band (e.g., Channel 1, 6, or 11 on 2.4 GHz).
- **Encryption Type:** WEP, WPA, WPA2, WPA3.

## 6. Hands-on / Lab Methodology
*Assuming this is an authorized lab environment testing a WPA2 network.*

- **What to observe first:** The physical environment and available wireless interfaces. Ensure your Wi-Fi adapter supports **Monitor Mode** and packet injection.
- **Attack surface:** The WPA2 4-way handshake between a connected client and the access point.
- **Initial hypothesis:** The network password is weak and can be cracked if we capture the handshake.
- **What to test:** Sending forged Deauthentication frames to a client to force them to disconnect and reconnect.
- **How to test it safely:**
  1. Put the adapter into Monitor Mode.
  2. Sniff traffic on the target AP's channel.
  3. Inject Deauth packets spoofing the AP's MAC address.
  4. Listen for the reconnection handshake.
- **Expected result:** The client momentarily disconnects, reconnects, and your sniffer captures the encrypted 4-way handshake (the `.cap` file).
- **How to interpret the result:** The captured handshake is an encrypted hash. You must now run an offline dictionary attack to guess the plaintext password.

## 7. Failed Attempts & Why
- **What was tried:** Running Wireshark on a standard laptop to capture passwords of other users on the Wi-Fi.
- **Why it failed:** The wireless adapter was in **Promiscuous Mode/Managed Mode**, meaning the hardware drops all packets not destined for its own MAC address on the wireless network.
- **What that tells us:** To sniff an open medium, the adapter must be configured to process all received RF frames, regardless of destination.
- **What to try next:** Switch the adapter to **Monitor Mode**.

## 8. Example
**IoT Botnets (e.g., Mirai Botnet):**
IoT devices (smart cameras, thermostats) often rely on wireless networks (Zigbee, Wi-Fi). They are notoriously insecure, using default passwords and lacking patch mechanisms. 
The Mirai malware scanned networks (wired and wireless), logged into these devices using default Telnet credentials, and conscripted hundreds of thousands of them into a hijacked network used to launch massive DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) attacks that took down major sections of the internet.

## 9. Impact
Because the medium is accessible to anyone in range:
- Weak protocols (WEP) can be cracked in minutes, granting full network access.
- Evil Twin attacks (setting up a fake AP with the same name) can trick users into connecting, allowing an attacker to intercept credentials and perform MitM attacks.
- Critical infrastructure using vulnerable RF can be monitored or disrupted.
- Hijacked IoT devices become launchpads for global DDoS attacks.

## 10. Detection
- **WIDS / WIPS:** Wireless Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems monitor the airwaves for anomalous behavior.
- **Rogue AP Detection:** Scanning for unexpected BSSIDs claiming to be the corporate network.
- **Deauth Floods:** High volumes of management frames often indicate an ongoing attack to capture handshakes or a denial of service.

## 11. Prevention / Remediation
- **WPA3:** Use the latest standard. It introduces **Protected Management Frames (PMF)**, meaning Deauth packets cannot be spoofed, preventing forced reconnections.
- **Strong Passphrases:** Protects WPA2 from offline dictionary attacks.
- **Disable WPS:** Wi-Fi Protected Setup PINs are easily brute-forced.
- **Faraday Cages/Bags:** Use conductive enclosures (like copper mesh) to block external RF signals. Crucial for pentesters to prevent seized mobile devices from being remotely wiped via cellular networks.
- **Change IoT Defaults:** Always update default passwords on smart devices and disable unnecessary services like Telnet.

## 12. Related Concepts
- **Networking Models (OSI Layer 1):** Wireless RF operates at the Physical layer, distinct from ethernet cables, but layers 2 and above operate similarly.
- **Cryptography (Hashing):** WPA2 cracking relies on understanding how the pre-shared key is hashed during the 4-way handshake, combining the password with the SSID (salt) and nonces.

## 13. Important Commands / Syntax
The `aircrack-ng` suite is a common toolset for assessing Wi-Fi security.

```bash
# 1. Puts the wireless interface (wlan0) into Monitor Mode.
sudo airmon-ng start wlan0

# 2. Sniffs all wireless traffic on a specific channel, saving to a file.
sudo airodump-ng -c 6 --bssid 00:11:22:33:44:55 -w capture_file wlan0mon

# 3. Injects Deauth packets (-0) to a specific client (-c) from the AP (-a).
sudo aireplay-ng -0 5 -a 00:11:22:33:44:55 -c AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF wlan0mon

# 4. Attempts to crack the captured handshake using a wordlist.
aircrack-ng -w wordlist.txt capture_file.cap
```

## 14. Interview Questions
**Q: What is the difference between Promiscuous Mode and Monitor Mode?**
> Promiscuous mode captures all traffic on a *wired* network segment that reaches the interface. Monitor mode captures all *wireless* traffic in the air on a specific channel, without needing to be connected to the network.

**Q: How does a Faraday Cage work?**
> It is an enclosure made of conductive material that distributes external electromagnetic fields across its exterior, blocking RF signals from entering or exiting. 

**Q: What is the primary vulnerability in IEEE 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) and earlier?**
> Management frames are unencrypted and unauthenticated. An attacker can spoof an AP's MAC address and send Deauth frames to disconnect a client.

**Q: Explain Modulation.**
> It is the process of altering a high-frequency carrier wave by mixing it with a message signal (the data). The carrier allows the signal to travel far, while the modulation encodes the information.

## 16. Key Takeaways
- The wireless medium is fundamentally open; physical building security does not prevent network sniffing.
- You must use **Monitor Mode** to capture ambient wireless traffic not destined for your machine.
- **WPA2** is robust but vulnerable to offline dictionary attacks if the handshake is captured and the password is weak.
- **WPA3** mitigates Deauth attacks via Protected Management Frames (PMF).
- **IoT devices** are prime targets for botnets due to weak default security and lack of patching.
- The **UHF band** (300-3000 MHz) encompasses most modern consumer wireless tech (Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz, Bluetooth, Cellular).

## 17. One-Minute Revision
**How It All Works Together:** Wireless networks broadcast data through the open air using modulated RF signals on standards like IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi). Because the medium is completely open, an attacker can use a Wi-Fi adapter in Monitor Mode to passively sniff traffic. By sending spoofed, unencrypted management frames (Deauthentication attacks), they can force clients to disconnect and capture their encrypted 4-way reconnection handshakes. Once a handshake is captured, attackers use offline dictionary attacks to uncover the password. From there, they can pivot into the network, often targeting weakly secured IoT devices to build botnets. Modern defenses rely on WPA3 (which encrypts management frames), strong passwords, and monitoring tools (WIDS).
