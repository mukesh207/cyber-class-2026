## 🛡️ Foundational Concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Event** | Any observable occurrence in a system or network (e.g., a user logging in, a firewall blocking a connection). |
| **Log** | A permanent or semi-permanent record of events occurring within an organization's systems and networks. |
| **Incident** | A security event that negatively affects the confidentiality, integrity, or availability (CIA triad) of information assets (e.g., a data breach). |

## 🛠️ The PTES Framework (Penetration Testing Execution Standard)

The PTES standard provides a comprehensive lifecycle for conducting a penetration test, ensuring consistency and quality across the industry.

### 1️⃣ Pre-engagement Interactions
This phase is all about understanding the client, the services they need, and clearly defining what we provide.
- **Scoping (In-Scope vs. Out-of-Scope):**
  - **In-Scope:** Systems, subnets, applications, or physical locations explicitly permitted to be tested.
  - **Out-of-Scope:** Assets that are strictly off-limits (e.g., a third-party payment gateway, a specific production database). Attacking these can lead to legal trouble.
- **NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement):**
  - **Why it matters:** A legal contract that establishes a confidential relationship.
  - **Legal Protection:** It protects the client's sensitive intellectual property and data from being leaked by the pentester. It also legally authorizes the pentester to perform "attacks," protecting the company from hacking charges.

### 2️⃣ Intelligence Gathering (Reconnaissance)
Gathering information about the target before launching an attack.
- **Passive Reconnaissance:** Gathering data without directly interacting with the target's infrastructure.
  - *Theoretical/Technical:* Querying public records (WHOIS), searching social media (OSINT), checking Shodan, or analyzing public DNS records. The target has no logs of you doing this.
- **Active Reconnaissance:** Directly interacting with the target's systems to map out infrastructure.
  - *Ping as Active Recon:* Running `ping <target>` sends an ICMP Echo Request directly to their server. Because the packet touches their network, their firewall or IDS can log your IP address. It is "active" because you are knocking on their door.

### 3️⃣ Threat Modeling
Taking the information gathered during recon and identifying the highest-risk threats and vulnerabilities. It helps pentesters prioritize their attack vectors based on the specific business logic and assets of the target.

### 4️⃣ Vulnerability Analysis
The process of discovering flaws in systems and applications.
- **Automated Analysis:** Using tools like Nessus or OpenVAS to quickly scan for known CVEs and misconfigurations. Good for coverage, but noisy.
- **Manual Analysis:** Deep-dive inspection by a human to find logical flaws, chained vulnerabilities, or zero-days that automated scanners miss.

### 5️⃣ Exploitation
The "hacking" phase. The tester takes advantage of the discovered vulnerabilities to gain unauthorized access, bypass security controls, or demonstrate the potential impact of a flaw (e.g., popping a reverse shell).

### 6️⃣ Post-Exploitation
What happens *after* you get in. The goal here is to determine the value of the compromised machine and maintain control.
- **Actions:** Privilege escalation (getting root/SYSTEM), dumping credentials, pivoting to other machines on the internal network, and establishing persistence.

### 7️⃣ Reporting
The most critical phase for the client. If it’s not in the report, it didn't happen.
- **Important Elements:** Executive summary for leadership, technical details for the IT team, methodology, and scope confirmation.
- **CVE & CVSS:**
  - **CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures):** Standardized identifiers for known vulnerabilities (e.g., CVE-2021-44228).
  - **CVSS (Common Vulnerability Scoring System):** A numerical score (0.0 to 10.0) reflecting the severity and impact of a vulnerability.
- **Remediation:** Actionable, specific steps the client must take to fix the vulnerability (e.g., "Update Apache to version 2.4.50" rather than just "Fix server").
- **Types of Testing / Standards:** Specifying what standard was followed (e.g., OWASP Top 10 for web apps, PCI-DSS for payment card environments, HIPAA for healthcare) validates the testing approach.

## 🎯 Interview Q&A

**Q: What is the difference between an Event and an Incident?**
> An event is just something that happens on a network (like a user logging in or a failed password attempt) and is recorded in a log. An incident is a specific event that causes harm or violates a security policy, like a successful unauthorized login leading to data exfiltration.

**Q: Why is ping considered Active Reconnaissance?**
> Because sending a ping (ICMP Echo Request) involves directly sending packets to the target's server. This interaction touches their network equipment, meaning the target's firewall or intrusion detection systems can log your IP address and detect your activity.

**Q: Why is an NDA and strict Scoping legally critical before a pentest?**
> Hacking a system without permission is a crime. The scoping document acts as legal authorization, explicitly defining what you are allowed to attack. The NDA ensures that any sensitive data you uncover remains confidential, protecting both the client's trade secrets and the pentester from legal liability.

**Q: What is the difference between Manual and Automated Vulnerability Analysis?**
> Automated analysis uses scanning software to quickly cross-reference assets against databases of known vulnerabilities (CVEs), but it can produce false positives and miss business logic flaws. Manual analysis relies on the pentester's expertise to find complex, multi-step vulnerabilities or custom application flaws that scanners are blind to.

## 📝 Summary / TL;DR

Today focused on the **PTES (Penetration Testing Execution Standard)** lifecycle. We emphasized that pentesting starts with strict legal boundaries via **NDAs and Scoping** during Pre-engagement. We explored how **Intelligence Gathering** transitions from untraceable **Passive Recon** to traceable **Active Recon** (like Ping). The lifecycle progresses through **Threat Modeling, Vulnerability Analysis, Exploitation, and Post-Exploitation**, culminating in the **Reporting** phase. A professional report must provide clear metrics like **CVE/CVSS** scores and actionable **Remediation** steps, often tailored to specific industry standards.
